# Side Quest Library

Optional quests for students who finish early, for warm-ups, and for short sessions between story chapters. Open **Side quest library** from Teacher controls (`/quest-library`), search, pick, and add quests to the game that is running. Every quest can be changed, and you can write your own (see *Writing and changing quests*).

## What is in it

| Kind | Theme | What it is | Where it fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Story | Castanyada | Ten quests for each chapter of The Haunted Castanyada. They use that chapter's people, places, times and clues. | Only while that chapter is running, in any Castanyada template that has the chapter. |
| Generic | Social | Social missions for fun: icebreakers, interviews, opinions, debates, story chains, describe-and-guess, secret agent missions, team decisions, kindness, hobbies, media, plans, travel, creative writing. | Any game, any chapter. |
| Generic | Plaza | Quests that use the plaza: directions, caf&eacute;/shop/hotel role-plays, events in the square and park, avatar fashion. | Any game on the plaza map. |
| Generic | Puzzles | Riddles, word games, logic and number puzzles, mostly with a typed answer the game checks. | Any game. |
| Generic | Seasonal | Autumn and Halloween, winter holidays and New Year, Carnival, Saint George's Day, spring and summer. | Any game, in the right season. |

Every quest card shows its kind (**Story** in orange, **Generic** in blue), its theme, a level and a short description of who it involves:

- **Levels:** A1 Starter, A2 Elementary, B1 Intermediate, B2 Advanced. The instructions themselves are written at the level, with sentence frames for A1 and useful phrases from A2.
- **Who:** solo, pair, small group or mingle (a quest done alone by talking to several classmates, called "whole class" before). A pair (2) or small group (3) plays together, formed by the game; a mingle needs 4 students online.
- **Answer:** a *typed answer* is checked by the game (riddles, puzzles, story facts); a *written report* receives an automatic task check and one short feedback message after submission. A clear omission keeps it open for revision; minor language mistakes can pass. You can read submitted writing under "What students wrote" in Teacher controls.
- **Success criteria (optional, private):** up to 600 characters of essential requirements and reference facts. Otherwise the check uses the student instructions. Teacher notes are not sent to the model.
- **Teacher note:** what to look for, how to adapt the quest, and for typed answers the solution and every accepted variant.

## Finding quests

- Type words in the search box: a topic (food, hotel, Christmas), a language point (past simple, comparatives, giving opinions), a level name (starter) or an activity (riddle, survey, role-play). Every word must match; title matches come first.
- Narrow with the filters. Each button shows how many quests it would leave. *Kind* separates story and generic quests; *Chapter* appears for story quests.
- Shortcuts: warm-ups (5 minutes or less), auto-checked answers, pair work, mingles, story quests for the running chapter, Halloween and autumn, Christmas and winter.
- Tick **Only quests that fit … now** to hide quests the running game cannot take (story quests for another chapter, or quests already added).
- Click a tag inside a teacher note to search for it.

## Adding quests to a game

1. In Teacher controls, create or open the game. A campaign is not needed: a new class can have side quests straight away.
2. In the library, select up to ten quests (or press **Pick 5 at random from these**) and choose **Review and add to the game**. The review shows who each quest is for and how many students could accept it right now.
3. Choose **Release to students**. The quests join the game's quest board and stay there for the whole game, whichever chapter a campaign is on, until you close or withdraw them. A campaign's progress is not touched.

A **story quest** continues one campaign chapter: it can be added only while that chapter runs, and it leaves the board when the next chapter starts. Generic quests work in any game, with or without a campaign.

## How students play them

- These rules hold for every quest on the board: library quests, quests released from Mission Studio, and the partner quests written in a mission (such as the Castanyada interviews, which keep their assigned witness and the witness exchange).
- **Open at once.** Side quests do not wait for a warm-up or for a campaign's main questions: a student can accept one whenever they like. Without a campaign the Journal shows only the quest board. A partner quest written in a mission keeps its own rule if its author set it to follow the main mystery.
- **Choose one when there is nothing else to do.** A student with no warm-up part left and no main questions left sees a *Choose a side quest* message, and the Journal opens at the quest board. In a class with only side quests, that is everyone at once. A student still on the warm-up or the campaign is never asked or placed.
- **One at a time.** While a student does a side quest, the others are out of sight. **Leave this quest and choose another** in the Journal gives it up and brings the choice back. After finishing one, they choose the next.
- **Pairs and groups are real.** Accepting a pair or small-group quest puts the student in the pair or group for it that has waited longest, or opens a new one ("Join Ana" on the card shows who is waiting; those come first). The quest starts once the group is full. After three minutes, a group with at least two members can choose **Start with who's here**. Members find each other in the plaza; **one answer, or one report, counts for everyone in the group**, and each gets the XP. Under *What students wrote* a group report shows who else was in it.
- **Accidental report submission.** A sent report stays visible in the Journal with **Undo submission**. The author can reopen individual writing; any group member can reopen their shared report for the whole original group, including members who are offline. The text comes back as a draft and leaves the teacher's submitted reports until it is sent again. A teammate can submit the revision, and completion XP is awarded only once. If somebody has already started another side quest, they must leave it before the group report can reopen. A mission report cannot reopen after the campaign ends or after a dependent task has started or finished.
- **Mingles** (called "whole class" before) are done alone, by talking to several classmates; they need 4 students online.
- **Nobody waits forever to choose.** A student asked to choose who has not chosen within a minute is placed by the game: in a pair or group that is waiting, or else in a solo quest or mingle.
- **Teacher controls** has a *Side quests* tab in every open game: who is free to choose, the pairs and groups (waiting or playing), who is doing which quest, how many are still on the warm-up or campaign, and what students wrote. It warns when students are free and the game has no side quests. **Place in a side quest…** next to a free student puts them in a quest (or its waiting group).
- **Close** (in the library, under *Added to this game*) stops new students taking a quest; those doing it can finish. **Withdraw** takes off a quest nobody has started.
- **Erase campaign** keeps the game's side quests, who did them and what they wrote: they belong to the game. Deleting the game removes them.

The server builds each quest from its own copy of the library, checks that a story quest fits the running chapter, and adds a quest once per game; a game holds up to 100. Side quests added to a campaign chapter by an older version move to their game when the server starts, with who finished them and who was doing them.

**Download for Mission Studio** saves the selection as a campaign file. In Mission Studio, import it with "Extend the selected chapter" to make the quests part of your own campaign draft.

## Writing and changing quests

- **+ New side quest** opens the editor beside the library. Fill in the title, what the student reads, theme, category, level, who, answer type and minutes; a typed answer also needs the accepted answers, one a line. The preview shows the quest as it appears on the quest board, and the editor says what is still missing. **Add to the library** saves it; the game gives it an ID from its title.
- **Edit** on a card changes that quest. **Duplicate** opens a copy to make a variant (an easier level, another topic). Games that already have a quest keep the version they got.
- A **story** quest belongs to one chapter. The editor lists the chapters of the published missions; it can be limited to some roles, unlock after a main question (its ID), or only after the student finishes the main mystery.
- **Delete** moves a quest to **Trash**, out of the library and out of reach of new releases. **Restore quest** in Trash brings it back.
- **Export** saves the quests you see (all, or those your search shows) as a file. **Import file** adds the quests in such a file and updates those with the same ID; nothing is imported from a file with a broken quest.
- Each quest is kept on the online Pixel Plaza and travels to and from the class laptop on its own, like a mission. A quest changed in both places while the laptop was offline is kept twice, the laptop's marked *(laptop)*.

## Plaza Free Play

A campaign without a mystery: students get one of four light roles (Explorer, Reporter, Host, Artist) and check in with one or two sentences each session. Side quests no longer need it, since any game can have them, so use it only if you want the check-ins. You can move it to Trash in Mission Studio.

## Good to know

- Students type in English: the chat blocks words that are not English, so quests about local festivals ask for English names ("chestnut festival", "Saint George's Day").
- Story quests never spoil a main answer: those that use information from a main question unlock after it, and those about a chapter's solution unlock once the student has finished the main mystery. None asks who the thief is, so the thief can play them too.
- Written reports are not graded automatically. Choose a few to discuss with the class.
- The quests are data on the online server, one record each, like missions, and the class laptop keeps a copy (README, *Missions live online*). `worker/sideQuestStore.ts` keeps them; on its first start it turns a library from before (one file) into records. `shared/sideQuests.ts` holds their shape, checks every quest from the editor, a file or the other server, turns quests into quest-board objectives and provides the search. The tests use a frozen copy in `worker/tests/fixtures/side-quests.json` and check every quest against the game's rules.
