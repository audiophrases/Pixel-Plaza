# The Haunted Castanyada: Five Chapters

Choose **The Haunted Castanyada: Five Chapters** in the teacher campaign list, or open `castanyada-complete` in Mission Studio to inspect, duplicate and edit it. This is a separate published template. The original three-chapter campaign and classes already in progress keep their existing revisions.

## Story and investigations

| Chapter | Shared investigation | Fast-finisher interview | Fast-finisher exchange |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The Missing Chestnuts | Find the box, reconstruct its journey and identify key holders | Audit the moon-labeled delivery using two witnesses' records | Negotiate a fair distribution of forty chestnuts |
| 2. Conflicting Stories | Meet the relevant roles and identify the thief | Explain inconsistent clocks and the automatic hotel bell | Correct a damaging rumor using checked facts |
| 3. The Hotel Cellar | Find the chestnuts, combine code fragments and discover the motive | Inspect cellar and decoration risks | Negotiate commitments that restore trust after the surprise |
| 4. The Missing Festival Plan | Find the latest red folder, distinguish old and new schedules, select the safe venue and submit a planning proposal | Check conflicting posters against two sources | Agree on a hall layout that balances both roles' needs |
| 5. The Haunted Celebration | Recover the lantern, explain the ghostly noise, identify the invited guests and propose an inclusive welcome | Investigate misunderstandings about the invitation | Agree on an optional welcome activity and write an English welcome message |

The morning theft leads into late-afternoon preparation. Rehearsal is at 5:30 and the party starts at 6:00. The moon parcel introduced in the first side investigation returns as the lantern in the finale. The thief mystery is resolved in Chapter 3; Chapters 4–5 do not select a new culprit.

## How the side quests work

There are ten social board quests, two per chapter. Students earn private notes for both activities when they finish that chapter's main mystery. The first quest is an interview; completing its report unlocks the second, an exchange with an agreed outcome. Each quest can be completed once by each investigator.

Both assigned investigators must be online, have finished their main mystery, and hold different roles. Students who are still working are not recruited as helpers. A quest waits when no eligible partner is available. A disconnected partner remains assigned and can be replaced through the existing journal control.

Each role contributes different evidence or a practical need. Prompts ask students to explain their note, ask a follow-up question, compare alternatives and record a finding or compromise. English sentence starters support asking, checking evidence and negotiating. Reports use a multiline box with a 1,000-character limit; ordinary short answers retain their 80-character limit.

Town notices supply missing-role clues at the corresponding earned stage. For a full roster, another online role's private note is not copied into an investigator's journal. Main investigations also have map discoveries and prepared teacher news. The teacher can advance explicitly; reaching the readiness threshold leaves time for unfinished main work and side investigations.

## Teacher review and classroom trial

Read reports and witness transcripts in the teacher campaign controls. Chapters 4–5 end their main path with a proposal; report submission counts as an attempt after five words, not an automatically graded decision. Social side reports now require a quest-linked question and reply, both with private evidence and teacher approval. The engine verifies those in-game events; the teacher assesses language, reasoning and any spoken conversation. Choose [Castanyada: Witness Investigations](witness-investigations.md) to require exchanges before the main conclusions as well.

Before advancing, select a few reports to discuss with the class: What did your partner tell you? Which evidence changed your mind? What compromise did you make? Use students' recommendations to make a classroom decision about the festival; proposals do not automatically move map objects or change decorations.

Trial with approximately twenty students and some absences. Observe time to finish the main path, time waiting for a different-role partner, use of English follow-up questions, quality of evidence and whether both partners contribute. Students may finish at different speeds; pair availability and the ten quests do not establish a fixed lesson duration. Adjust report prompts, role notes and chapter timing in Studio after observing the class.
