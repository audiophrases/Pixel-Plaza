# Castanyada witness investigations

In Teacher controls, start **Castanyada: Witness Investigations** for a new game. This five-chapter template requires an approved exchange before each main conclusion; the thief question requires replies from two distinct witnesses. Existing saved campaigns keep their pinned main objectives. Mission Studio can also require one or two witnesses on individual objectives.

The student's journal shows the question, eligible online witnesses and their own private clues. The investigator chooses a witness, writes a question about the mystery and attaches a private clue. The question goes through the existing chat moderation queue. Once the teacher approves it, the witness sees the question and evidence in their journal, writes a reply and shares one of their own unlocked private clues. The reply also requires approval. Only then can the investigator submit a conclusion. Two-witness tasks require at least three students; one-witness tasks require at least two. There is no automatic solo bypass.

Interview and information-exchange board quests require the same exchange with the assigned partner before a report counts, including in existing campaigns. Both students contribute private evidence. A report of at least five words still counts as a written attempt after participation is verified. The game does not grade language or reasoning. Teachers should approve relevant questions and replies and assess the conclusion themselves.

Ordinary chat, self-interviews, guessed answers, fabricated clue IDs and another student's reply do not earn participation. The server supplies the attached evidence from the sender's actual journal. Unapproved witness evidence stays hidden from the investigator, and other classmates cannot see the transcript. Exchanges are scoped to a game, chapter, objective and investigator, so they cannot be reused for another question. A replacement partner requires a new exchange with that partner.

Teacher controls includes **Witness exchanges** under the campaign. It shows the task, participants, question, reply, evidence and approval status. Questions and replies remain in the chat queue for approval, rejection or removal. Removing an approved turn revokes its participation credit for an unfinished conclusion; it does not erase conclusions already submitted. Rejected questions can be rewritten, and rejected or removed replies can be revised by the original witness.

Approved exchanges and reports survive reconnects and server restarts. A conclusion remains available if a witness goes offline after the exchange. Closing the class rejects pending turns; resetting a campaign or deleting its game clears its exchanges. These records demonstrate an in-game exchange, not spoken conversation or comprehension. Classroom pacing and the relevance of student contributions still need teacher observation.
