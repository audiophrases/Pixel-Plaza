# The Trophy That Walked — teacher guide

**Spoilers.** Four short lessons; the main learning activity is describing a picture and asking for missing information. The apparent theft is a mix-up: a robot has a fake cup, a student protected the real one, and a teacher hid a party invitation in its speaker.

## Before each chapter

Read the short opening. Ask: “What are we trying to find out?” Take one answer, then start. Repeat only: **look, tell, answer**. Students keep their own screens and describe their pictures in English. Open Catalan help when needed.

Use teams of four where possible. Each question names the two roles whose clues fit together. The role with the symbol or code asks; the role with the key answers. Everyone then shares the result in Team chat. A useful exchange is: “I see a book sign. What place is that?” / “The book means library.” / “So the duck went to the library.”

Allow 2 minutes for the opening, 6–8 for picture chats and optional exploration, 4–6 for the shared report, and 2–4 for the reveal. Time estimates are guidance. Use teacher-controlled chapter advancement if you want a shared reveal; check the game’s automatic advancement setting first.

## Chat, then report

Every chapter has two short-answer questions and a three-part or two-part report. Tasks unlock in order. Students ask the provided question in Team chat, describe their pictures and agree on the answer. One teammate submits their report in the Journal. This uses the same direct Team chat flow as Season 2; there is no separate witness form or interview approval. If case teams are off, use normal class chat and have each student submit their own answers.

Demonstrate one exchange. Ask students to describe their own picture before the team submits. Chatting is supported by the information split and repeated prompts; the software does not count or require a number of Team chat messages. Keep reports short; the task provides the sentence starters. Review uncertain/unavailable report checks in Teacher controls. Do not insist on exact wording or extra facts that only appear in endings.

## Reports and reveals

1. Report: “The duck went to the library. It belongs to Laia.” Reveal: the duck is a robot. Laia says the cup is not the real trophy. Do not show the cardboard-cup illustration yet.
2. Report: “One has a circle. The other has a star. We think they are different cups.” Reveal: the duck’s cup is cardboard. Chidi moved the real trophy at 9:00, before the duck at 9:05. Ask: “Does moving it mean stealing it?”
3. Report: “Chidi moved it because it was getting wet. It is safe in first aid.” Reveal: he saved it. Then the trophy speaks. Ask: “How can a trophy talk?” The rescue illustration appears now, after students have identified the room and reason.
4. Report: “Come to the courtyard. The message is from Ms Diop. It plays through a speaker.” Reveal: the message is an invitation to the school party. The duck throws confetti. Ask: “Which surprise was the best? Why?”

The trophy’s voice is written story dialogue, not an audio feature. The box, robot and confetti are illustrated props, not interactive map objects. Students walk to safe clue zones outside buildings. Staff handle repairs; never ask students to enter the blocked passage.

## Keep the plot clear

Only three names matter: Laia builds the robot, Chidi moves the real trophy, Ms Diop plans the party. The real trophy has a STAR. The fake cup has a ROUND badge. Symbols have labels and text equivalents, so colour or image recognition alone is never required.

The leak and trophy move were not part of the party plan. The duck was carrying its own fake cup for a show, not secretly swapping trophies. Ms Diop put the speaker in the real trophy before it was moved. This explains all four surprises without a new villain or an extra hidden event.

Do not show students the full teacher preview, pack JSON, folder or this guide: these contain future clues. Use the app’s normal private views and chapter endings. Standalone chapter files include the current role clues and their own ending, so an early chapter does not show the campaign finale.
