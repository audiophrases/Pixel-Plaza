# Protocol 8:15 — teacher guide (spoilers)

The supplied three-chapter storyline is the narrative source. Short records and witness accounts turn its events into discoverable evidence. Laia’s screenshot, Chidi’s observation, Sokolova’s dated notice and the adults’ admissions all matter. Nobody is a randomly assigned thief. Investigator roles hold information about the fixed story characters.

## Each lesson: 20–25 minutes

Allow 3 minutes to read the opening and role clue, 7–9 minutes to explore safe evidence points and compare information, 5–7 minutes for the witness exchange and short report, and 3–5 minutes to discuss the cliffhanger. Optional writing is for early finishers. Pacing is guidance, not a timer.

Each chapter has two short-answer questions followed by a short report. Tasks open one at a time. Reports require one approved witness exchange through **Choose a witness** in the Journal; ordinary chat does not complete this step. Students can use the sentence starters in each task. Accept short, simple sentences that include the required facts; there is no target word count. If checks are unavailable or uncertain, review the saved report in Teacher controls. The 70% readiness threshold does not advance the chapter; the teacher does.

## Say this before play

“Karina is missing after a storm. You are detectives. Find out what happened together.”

1. Open the Journal. Read your clue.
2. Tell your team: “My clue says…” Ask: “What does yours say?”
3. Follow the current task. Read the clue at that place.
4. Talk, then answer in the Journal. In teams, one answer counts for everyone.

For a report, demonstrate **Choose a witness** once: choose a classmate, select a clue, send a question, and have them reply with their clue. Wait for the exchange to be approved, then submit the report. Use the actual students as witnesses; Karina and the teachers are story characters, not players to find on the map. Stay outside the blocked passage; staff handle the danger.

Before students move, ask one student to say the mission and another to say the first step. Keep the spoken explanation to those two things. Explain later steps when they open.

## Chapter 1 — The Missing Student

Opening: 8:15 announcements contradict each other; Karina is absent at 8:16. Learners distinguish observations from accusations.

1. Place in both messages: **sports hall**. Evidence: message checker’s Laia screenshot and the office printout. One message says to stay in classrooms and avoid the sports hall; the other says to go there.
2. What fell: **roof panel**. Evidence: place checker’s Chidi account and the safe sports-hall evidence point.
3. Report: two supported facts and one unanswered question. Require an evidence exchange, not a culprit. “Where is Karina?” and “Who posted the second message?” are legitimate unresolved questions.
4. Optional: write a rumour-correcting group-chat message. This is a writing exercise in the Journal.

Cliffhanger: CCTV shows Ms Diop entering the sports hall with Karina at 8:07, contradicting her denial. The clip does not show the back exit. Do not release Chapter 2 explanations early.

## Chapter 2 — Why Did Ms Diop Lie?

Opening: Ms Diop lied about seeing Karina. The question is whether she was helping her. The clues introduce Ionescu’s defence and Youssef’s decision to leave Karina alone.

1. Warning author: **Ms Awa Diop**. Evidence: document checker’s clue and the library notice.
2. Office receipt time: **09:45 on Friday**. The report was written at 09:20; the receipt at 09:45 proves management had it. The question asks for the time, not the day.
3. Report: use two sources to reconsider blaming Diop. Accept evidence of the prior warning, receipt, Youssef’s admission or Ionescu’s account; students do not yet need to know where Karina is or who ordered the cover-up.
4. Optional: complete a short apology from Youssef and say what he will do next time.

Cliffhanger: at 8:28 Karina’s account sends a simplified message: she is safe, and someone told Diop to keep quiet. Do not treat account identity alone as proof of location. Chapter 3 confirms it.

## Chapter 3 — The Truth

1. Karina’s location: **first-aid room**. Evidence: message checker’s clue, Ionescu’s account and the staff statement outside first aid.
2. Person ordering concealment: **Mr El Idrissi**, the deputy head. Evidence: saved emails at the office plus Diop’s admission. A document and a witness account support the conclusion.
3. Report: identify the unsafe covered passage; cite two facts; recommend one safe action for staff. Examples: maintain closure, verify attendance, arrange repair, preserve records. Learners should not enter or repair the damaged area.
4. Optional: choose Youssef or Ms Diop and say how they should help now, with a reason.

Ending: Karina is safe; Ms Diop helped her; El Idrissi delayed repairs and told her to hide the truth. Ask the class: “Was Ms Diop right to lie?” Accept different opinions with reasons.

The borrowed laptop explains how Karina can send the Chapter 2 message while her phone is broken. The unfilmed back exit explains how the CCTV can be authentic without proving wrongdoing. The evidence establishes El Idrissi’s concealment; it does not establish who typed each emergency announcement, so do not demand that answer.

## Language support

Encourage students to try the English, expand Catalan if needed, then speak and write in English. Translations reproduce the evidence without extra answers. Useful optional spoken frames: “The record says…”, “That does not prove…”, “What did your witness see?”, “We know… but we still do not know…”.

Short-answer tasks name the place to visit and the answer to write. Reports use a question for a classmate and sentence starters. Each opening gives the immediate story problem and a first action; later clues explain the rest. Role names use everyday words: message checker, people checker, document checker and place checker.
