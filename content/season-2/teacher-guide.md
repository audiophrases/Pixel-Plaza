# Protocol 8:15 — teacher guide (spoilers)

The supplied three-chapter storyline is the narrative source. Short records and witness accounts turn its events into discoverable evidence. Laia’s screenshot, Chidi’s observation, Sokolova’s dated notice and the adults’ admissions all matter. Nobody is a randomly assigned thief. Investigator roles hold information about the fixed story characters.

## Each lesson: 20–25 minutes

Allow 3 minutes to read the opening and role clue, 7–9 minutes to explore safe evidence points and compare information, 5–7 minutes for the witness exchange and short report, and 3–5 minutes to discuss the cliffhanger. Optional writing is for early finishers. Pacing is guidance, not a timer.

Each chapter has two typed questions followed by a main report. Each later main step waits for the previous one. Reports require one witness exchange through the Journal; they should be short (roughly 25–45 English words), not essays. The report checker has private success criteria. If checks are unavailable or uncertain, review the saved report in Teacher controls. The 70% readiness threshold does not advance the chapter; the teacher does.

## Chapter 1 — The Missing Student

Opening: 8:15 announcements contradict each other; Karina is absent at 8:16. Learners distinguish observations from accusations.

1. Conflicting destination: **sports hall**. Evidence: media analyst’s Laia screenshot and the office printout. One message says to stay in classrooms and avoid the sports hall; the other says to go there.
2. Obstruction: **fallen roof panel**. Evidence: site observer’s Chidi account and the safe sports-hall evidence point.
3. Report: two supported facts and one unanswered question. Require an evidence exchange, not a culprit. “Where is Karina?” and “Who posted the second message?” are legitimate unresolved questions.
4. Optional: write a rumour-correcting group-chat message. This is a writing exercise in the Journal.

Cliffhanger: CCTV shows Ms Diop entering the sports hall with Karina at 8:07, contradicting her denial. The clip does not show the back exit. Do not release Chapter 2 explanations early.

## Chapter 2 — Everyone Has a Secret

Opening: the CCTV creates suspicion. Ionescu defends Diop. Youssef admits abandoning Karina’s attempt to report a safety problem.

1. Warning author: **Ms Awa Diop**. Evidence: records researcher’s copy from Ms Sokolova and the library notice.
2. Office receipt time: **09:45 on Friday**. The report was written at 09:20; the receipt at 09:45 proves management had it. The question asks for the time, not the day.
3. Report: use two sources to reconsider blaming Diop. Accept evidence of the prior warning, receipt, Youssef’s admission or Ionescu’s account; students do not yet need to know where Karina is or who ordered the cover-up.
4. Optional: what should Youssef say to Karina now, and why?

Cliffhanger: at 8:28 Karina’s account sends the supplied message: she is safe, and someone told Diop to keep quiet. Do not treat account identity alone as proof of location. Chapter 3 supplies corroboration.

## Chapter 3 — The Truth

1. Karina’s location: **first-aid room**. Evidence: media analyst’s message record, Ionescu’s account and the staff statement outside first aid.
2. Person ordering concealment: **Mr El Idrissi**, the deputy head. Evidence: saved emails at the office plus Diop’s admission. A document and a witness account support the conclusion.
3. Report: identify the unsafe covered passage; cite two facts; recommend one safe action for staff. Examples: maintain closure, verify attendance, arrange repair, preserve records. Learners should not enter or repair the damaged area.
4. Optional: choose Youssef, Diop or Ionescu and propose a reasoned action to rebuild trust. Accept different defensible opinions.

Ending: Karina is safe; the cover-up is exposed; the adults speak honestly; the class discusses whether it would speak up at a personal cost.

The borrowed laptop explains how Karina can send the Chapter 2 message while her phone is broken. The unfilmed back exit explains how the CCTV can be authentic without proving wrongdoing. The evidence establishes El Idrissi’s concealment; it does not establish who typed each emergency announcement, so do not demand that answer.

## Language support

Encourage students to try the English, expand Catalan if needed, then speak and write in English. Translations reproduce the evidence without extra answers. Useful optional spoken frames: “The record says…”, “That does not prove…”, “What did your witness see?”, “We know… but we still do not know…”.

All 12 task instructions are under 25 English words; openings are under 40. The longer material is split into individual evidence cards. This retains suspense while reducing the reading load compared with Season 1.
